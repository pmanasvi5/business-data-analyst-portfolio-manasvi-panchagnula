## Business Problem

VT PUMP mentors, mentees, and program administrators needed a clearer way to find and use mentorship resources. Information spread across different locations can increase navigation effort, make onboarding inconsistent, and create extra maintenance work for program staff.

The team’s challenge was to organize the program’s materials into one usable Canvas experience while supporting the distinct needs of mentors and mentees.

## Objective

Create a centralized, easy-to-navigate mentorship platform with separate mentor and mentee pathways, organized resources, quick links, and short learning videos. The experience also needed to remain understandable and maintainable for program administrators.

## Data and Evidence

- **Source:** Exported Canvas course materials, project files, permitted videos, screenshots, and retrospective project context
- **Scope:** A nine-person student team, two audience-specific learning tracks, five curated resources, and 19 instructional videos in the recovered export
- **Important evidence:** Page structure, navigation, learning resources, instructional media, stakeholder-driven revisions, and my leadership and scheduling contribution
- **Privacy:** The portfolio version excludes private Canvas identifiers, student information, team email addresses, and restricted links

## Process

### 1. Define

The team considered three primary audiences: mentors, mentees, and program administrators. We organized the work around the information each group needed, the actions they needed to take, and the importance of clear navigation. I served as team lead and schedule manager for both subteams, coordinating work across the mentor and mentee sides of the experience.

### 2. Prepare

We gathered program resources, separated content by audience, organized quick links and learning materials, and coordinated production of instructional content. For this portfolio reconstruction, the exported Canvas package was converted into a standalone website so the structure and videos can be reviewed without requiring Canvas access.

### 3. Analyze

This was a project-delivery and business-analysis effort rather than a statistical analysis. Evaluation focused on whether user needs were traceable to concrete pages, resources, videos, or navigation choices. Claims about usage or adoption are not included because measured engagement data is not available.

### 4. Visualize and Communicate

The reconstructed website presents the project as a guided case study with mentor and mentee pathways and a playable learning-video library. It is designed so recruiters can understand the problem, delivery approach, outputs, and lessons quickly.

## Interactive Website

The standalone VT PUMP website is published and its 19 videos have been converted and tested for browser playback. [Open the live interactive website](https://manasvi-vt-bridge-program.pmanasvi5.chatgpt.site).

## Key Findings

1. **Audience-specific organization:** Separating mentor and mentee pathways created a clearer structure for groups with different tasks and resources.
2. **Reusable instructional content:** Short videos and organized quick links turned repeated guidance into resources participants could revisit.
3. **Maintainability mattered:** The platform needed to be useful to participants while remaining organized enough for program staff to update and hand off.

## Recommendations

- Maintain a simple content calendar with an owner for periodic link and video reviews.
- Add lightweight mentor and mentee feedback prompts to identify confusing or outdated materials.
- Track privacy-safe engagement measures before making future claims about adoption or impact.

## Results and Impact

The team delivered a centralized Canvas mentorship resource with separate mentor and mentee areas, organized links and documents, and 19 recovered instructional videos. My contribution included leading and scheduling both subteams. The evidence supports these delivered outputs and the design rationale; adoption, time savings, and engagement improvement were not measured.

## Challenges and Trade-offs

The work required balancing useful detail with simple navigation and coordinating two related subteams without losing consistency. Portfolio publication also required converting a Canvas-specific export into a standalone format while preserving playable media and excluding private platform details.

## What I Learned

I learned that schedule management is closely tied to requirements clarity: unclear ownership or dependencies quickly affect content delivery. I also learned to design around distinct user groups while maintaining one coherent system, and to separate delivered outputs from unmeasured impact when documenting a project.
